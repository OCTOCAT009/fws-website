Interactive Website Design.

Sites people do something in, rather than scroll past. The difference between a brochure and an experience is whether the visitor is a participant.

Interactive is not the same as animated

Animation is something the site does to you. Interaction is something you do to the site. One is a film; the other is a place. Most agencies sell the first and call it the second.

Interaction is worth building when participation is the point — when you want someone to feel they earned access, discovered something, or made a choice that changed what they saw.

What we build

  • Gated entries — a front door that filters for the audience the work is actually for
  • Gamified systems — points, rituals and progress built into browsing rather than bolted on
  • WebGL and shader work — real-time graphics, written rather than imported
  • Configurators and builders — let people assemble the thing they are buying
  • Experimental navigation — drifting, spatial and non-linear layouts, where they suit the brand

When not to do this

If your visitor arrives knowing what they want and just needs to find it, interaction is friction and you should not pay for it. A restaurant needs a menu and a booking button, not a WebGL entry gate.

Interaction pays off when the visitor is deciding whether they care, not where to click. That is a smaller set of businesses than the people selling it will admit.

Common questions

What counts as an interactive website?
Anything where the visitor changes what they see — a gate they have to pass, a system that tracks progress, a configurator, a real-time graphic they can move through. If the only interaction is scrolling and clicking links, it is a normal website with animation.
How much does an interactive website cost?
Flagship starts at ₹32,500 and covers custom backend and bespoke interactive systems. Simple interactive touches on a standard build cost far less; a full WebGL experience costs more. The variable is how much of it is genuinely new code.
Does interactivity hurt SEO?
It can, badly, if content only exists after a user action — a crawler will not answer five questions to get in. We keep indexable content in the HTML and put interaction on top of it, so the page can rank even when the experience is gated.
